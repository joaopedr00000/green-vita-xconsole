# GreenVita 0.3.3 latency lab (experimental)

Based on the exact 0.3.2 Final tag. Stable release remains unchanged. No bitrate, resolution, compressed queue capacity/order, decoder implementation, audio policy or RAW controls changes. The VPK uses the same title ID, so installing replaces the app; preserve the stable VPK and settings for rollback.

## Test modes
- Classic: preserves 0.3.2 presentation scheduling and output wait policy.
- Adaptive (compatibility option): decoder signals completed frame via Notify; main loop can wake early after a cadence-dependent minimum gap, and can wait at most 2ms (requested timeout) for an in-flight frame near a presentation opportunity. Avoid redundant SDL swaps if no new frame and unchanged cached overlay. At 50+ estimated fps, shorten the pending output wait to 4ms when AU age >=18ms with at least one more AU queued. Normal 30fps policy retained.
- Extreme (global default): earlier wake opportunity (12ms for fast stream), requested late wait up to 3ms, aggressive 2ms output wait for an already-late AU, including no later queued AU for fast streams. May trade pacing for freshness. Optional HIGH decoder thread priority on existing USER_2 core, set at worker creation; reconnect between tests. RTC already TIME_CRITICAL on USER_1; no further elevation, audio and input priorities unchanged. No clock changes, no extra textures, no new GPU synchronization assumptions. The requested timers are not guaranteed scheduling precision.

All modes retain normal maintenance deadline and input/overlay handling. A ready signal is revalidated against pending state; no decoder blocking on async render. No change to physical screen refresh or SDL/GXM scanout. CPU-side presentation interval is not display vblank.

## Compare
Same title, region, network, quality profile and scene. Restart stream after each mode change. Warm up 60s and play 5–10 minutes per mode. Compare pipeline average/p95, frame interval p95, actual FPS, output replacements, errors/resync, audio and subjective smoothness. HUD technical view shows mode, wake count, swaps saved and p95. p95 is nearest rank of up to 256 successful telemetry samples per reporting window, not a full-session percentile. Counters are session-local. Keep classic mode or reinstall 0.3.2 if artifacts, frame drops, audio glitches or controls regress.

## Real button-to-photon test
Use an external camera at 240fps or highest available, framing both the physical button and Vita display (not screen capture only). Same game, server region and network; choose an immediate visible, repeatable response such as opening a game menu. Record at least 20 presses per version/mode, spaced apart. Count frames from first visible button actuation to first visible screen response. At 240fps each frame is 4.167ms; physical button actuation is approximated by visible motion, with camera exposure/rolling shutter uncertainty. Report median and p95, not only best result. Repeat 0.3.2 and 0.3.3 classic/adaptive/extreme. Do not add RTT to local pipeline and claim total input latency. Do not claim total button-to-photon latency from HUD readings. See the hardware observations below.

## Extreme default promotion — same 0.3.3 experimental version
The tester supplied three physical Vita HUD captures in Extreme mode from build #45767246467. Instantaneous local pipeline: 3.84, 4.35, 4.54 ms (arithmetic mean 4.24 ms); window P95: 13.6, 12.9, 12.8 ms; reported presentation rate 61/60/60 fps; zero reported video discards or decoder errors in all three captures. The tester reports excellent image quality and perceptibly lower input lag. These are short, non-controlled samples, not total latency or a full-session mean; the actual panel refresh is not established by the FPS counter. Frame interval P95 of 23.2, 25.5 and 22.3 ms still warrants long-session pacing validation.

Extreme now defaults globally for every streaming quality profile. The old experimental `latency_mode` persisted field is retired, and `global_pacing_mode` stores future explicit compatibility choices. Older settings files (including those with Adaptive selected) therefore migrate to Extreme without deleting unrelated preferences. Explicit subsequent selections of Classic or Adaptive remain available and persist. Invalid values normalize to Extreme. Reconnect after a change to apply the existing decoder-thread priority policy. This change does not modify the tested scheduling algorithm, codec, resolution, bitrate, input or audio. Stable v0.3.2 release is untouched. Extended validation and external-camera button-to-photon measurement remain outstanding.

## Consistency refinement (development build, no release)
Extreme remains global default. Codec, bitrate, resolution and compressed AU order/capacity unchanged. Ready frames in cached Extreme playback at 50–75fps may receive a requested 0–2ms guard against short presentation bursts; compare pipeline and interval P95. Extreme audio starts at 40/40/60ms (LowLatency/Balanced/Quality), can decrease by 5ms per clean 30s window toward floors 40/40/50ms only with observed SDL queue low-water >=20ms, recovers +10ms to 110ms after underrun. Compatibility modes retain old target policy. No forced PCM deletion; existing cap behavior retained. SDL queue occupancy excludes hardware buffers and is not A/V offset. Batch-arrival EMA measures local delivery. RAW changes wake RTC immediately with existing 1ms fallback; new timing is from observed change to accepted data-channel dispatch, not physical actuation or server response. Stale neutral retries until accepted. NACK grace adapts 12–18ms only after >=16 unrequested recoveries; no RTP assembly or NACK repeat/expiry change. Expired/untracked gaps are not proof of permanent loss. Track missing telemetry and use bounded 5s logs for long tests. Existing affinity and priority preserved, no overclock. No proof of reduced power consumption, actual A/V synchronization, or long-session stability yet. 1ms total pipeline is not established and is below observed ~2ms decoder call alone. Compare 30–60min and external-camera button-to-photon. No stable release.

Follow-up guards: LowLatency profile and IDR recovery always retain the 12ms NACK grace. Extreme avoids only the redundant post-render SDL controller read when RAW is fresher than 8ms and not suppressed; ordinary main-loop/periodic SDL sampling and event pumping remain. Logs include seconds since video receiver creation for soak-test comparisons. CPU/power savings have not been measured.

## Extreme fast-path restoration (development build)
Six tester screenshots after consistency refinement: local 5.90/8.53/12.39/4.18/4.33/3.90ms (mean 6.54ms), window pipeline p95 18.2/18.2/17.9/14.0/14.3/14.0ms; interval p95 24.7/27.8/27.7/22.9/25.5/24.9ms. One accumulated video discard, no decode errors, 59–61 reported presentation FPS. RTT varied 22.18–47.94ms. Extra burst guard was invoked 1280 times by last capture. Remove guard entirely, retain frame-ready scheduling and global Extreme default. These are not controlled samples and causality is unproven. RAW lock contention is now distinguished from actual unavailability; defer SDL override for one pump when last RAW dispatch was fresh (<8ms), preserve actual fallback, View/Guide and stale-neutral paths. HUD reports last accepted normal input method, not lock availability. Edge acknowledgement uses the short state lock to avoid duplicate sends on contention. Audio rate correction in Extreme only: when SDL queue >= target+12ms, shorten newly decoded PCM by 1%, >=target+25ms by 2%, with in-place stereo linear interpolation; startup unchanged, cap and underrun recovery unchanged. This is a small speed/pitch adjustment, not pitch-preserving time stretch; check audible quality and actual queue. Counter reports cumulative trimmed PCM duration, not latency saved. RTC avoids intermediate pulse allocation and redundant expiry scans. No changes to compressed video ordering, texture count, bitrate, resolution, decoder, or clocks. Compare sustained P95, audio queue, input and subjective smoothness. No stable release.

## Direct wake and 60-second benchmark
Extreme cached playback can claim a ready texture and submit it immediately after a decoder wake instead of re-entering application/UI maintenance first. This shortcut uses a nonblocking mutex claim, the same two-texture ownership protocol, no extra textures and no new VSync/clock changes. The existing early-wake minimum gap is bypassed only on the cached Extreme shortcut; at most one direct presentation per maintenance iteration, then immediately service normal events, audio and input. This may increase short presentation bursts; compare interval P95/P99 and visible smoothness. Classic/Adaptive unchanged. Existing wait slices and late-wait fallback retained; no claims about OS timer resolution. Automatic benchmark runs on every uninterrupted live session: first presented frame starts 10s warmup, then 60s wall-clock collection of all successful CPU-side presented frames, independent of the short-window telemetry lock. Reset on pause, background, session/output or mode/profile change. Bounded 8192 samples per metric, preallocated; exact min/max/mean of all samples, nearest-rank median/P95/P99 of retained samples; omitted count disclosed. Sort once after the window. HUD page Benchmark 60s and deferred connection-status log with pipeline/interval/claim distributions. The result excludes RTP assembly before AU enqueue, network/server and physical scanout; no claim of 0.1ms total latency. No release. Audio correction, native input and codec remain unchanged.
